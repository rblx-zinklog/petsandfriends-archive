Rabbits look as though they live on carrots and dandelions, but a healthy rabbit diet is plainer than the cartoons suggest. Understanding the basics will save you money, mess and a good deal of worry.

Hay comes first

The bulk of a rabbit's diet should be fresh grass or good-quality hay, available at all times. Constant chewing keeps their teeth worn to a healthy length and keeps their digestion moving. If your rabbit seems uninterested in hay, that is worth mentioning to a vet.

Greens, pellets and treats

  • Leafy greens: a daily handful of washed greens, introduced one at a time so you can see what suits your rabbit.
  • Pellets: a small, measured portion according to the packet. They are a supplement, not the main meal.
  • Treats: fruit and carrots are sugary and should be occasional extras.
  • Water: always fresh, always available.

Patience

Change a rabbit's diet slowly, over a week or more. Rabbits have sensitive stomachs, and a sudden switch is a common cause of trouble. Watch their droppings, their appetite and their energy, and you will learn to read their health the way you read a favourite book.

This is a general overview, not veterinary advice. A rabbit that stops eating or producing droppings needs to see a vet promptly.
