There is a kind of friendship that does not require anyone to speak. Most people who have lived with an animal know it well: the dog who rests his chin on your foot while you work, the cat who chooses the arm of your chair and nothing else, the bird who sings only when you are in the room.

We tend to call these relationships simple, as though simplicity were a lesser thing. I have come to think the opposite. Between people, friendship is so often carried by words, and words can be clumsy. An animal friend asks for none of them. What they ask for is presence, which is harder than it sounds and rarer than it should be.

Being there

Sit with a dog for an hour and you will notice how little is demanded of you. Nothing needs to be solved. No opinion needs to be offered. You are simply required to remain, and in remaining you are given a great deal: warmth, rhythm, a sense that the afternoon is exactly as long as it ought to be.

To be accompanied without being questioned is one of the great luxuries of life.

What they teach us

If animals teach us anything, I suspect it is how to be a better friend to one another. Pay attention. Notice when someone is tired. Come when you are called, and sometimes when you are not. Be glad, openly, at the sound of a familiar step in the hall.

These are not grand lessons. But a great many good lives are built from small, repeated kindnesses, and the animals in our homes practise them every single day.
