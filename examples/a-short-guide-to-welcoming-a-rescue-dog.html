Bringing a rescue dog home is one of the kindest things a person can do, and also one of the most easily rushed. This guide is a gentle outline of what to prepare and what to expect. Every dog is different, so treat it as a starting point rather than a rulebook.

Before they arrive

  • Choose one quiet corner for a bed and water bowl, away from doors and loud appliances.
  • Put away shoes, cables and anything chewable you would be sad to lose.
  • Ask the shelter or rescue what food the dog has been eating, and begin with that.
  • Agree as a household on the rules: where the dog sleeps, which furniture is allowed, who walks when.

The first week

Keep everything calm and small. Show the dog the corner, the garden and the water, and then let them be. Resist the urge to introduce every friend and relative at once; a nervous dog is quietly counting exits, and too many new faces makes that counting harder.

Keep to a simple routine. Meals, walks and bedtime at roughly the same times each day tell a dog more about their new life than any amount of talking.

Patience

Many rescue dogs seem perfectly settled for a few days and then show their real personality. This is normal. A common rule of thumb is that dogs need days to decompress, weeks to learn a routine and months to feel truly at home.

If your dog seems unwell, frightened to a worrying degree or aggressive, ask a veterinarian or a qualified trainer for advice. This guide is general information only.
