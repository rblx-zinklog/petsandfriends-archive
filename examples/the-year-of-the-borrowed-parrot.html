When Mrs. Aldous from number nine announced that she was going abroad for a year, she handed me a cage, a bag of seed and a list of instructions, and she left before I had finished saying yes.

The parrot's name was Admiral. He was green, enormous in personality and not at all grateful. On his first morning in my kitchen he looked me up and down and said, in a perfect imitation of Mrs. Aldous, “Well, that won't do.”

The Admiral's opinions

Over the following weeks I learned that Admiral had views on everything. He disapproved of my toast. He disapproved of the radio unless it was playing something with trumpets. He greeted the postman with a wolf whistle and the vicar with a long, ominous silence.

But he also learned. By midsummer he could say my name. By autumn he had picked up the kettle's whistle and would announce tea a full minute before it was ready, which was irritating and, I admit, quite useful.

The year went by faster than I expected. When Mrs. Aldous returned, tanned and cheerful, Admiral looked at her for a long moment, turned to me and said, quite clearly, “Well, that won't do.”

I laughed so hard I had to sit down. And when she carried him home, I found, to my great surprise, that the kitchen had never been so quiet.
